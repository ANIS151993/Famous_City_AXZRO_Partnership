01 — Where the business stands

The fleet isn't the problem.
The front door is.

Famous City Limousine has the vehicles, the chauffeurs, the TCP license, and a genuinely good reputation across the Bay Area. What it doesn't have is a way for a business to buy from it. Here is what we found looking at the operation from the outside, the way a hotel's procurement team would.

Booking

Reservations leave your website

"Book Now" and "Contact" both send visitors to an external reservation system on a different domain. You lose the visitor, the design, the data, and the chance to follow up.

Sales

No account, no contract, no portal

A concierge who wants a standing arrangement has no way to open one. There's no rate card, no net-30 terms, no way to add authorized bookers. So they call a competitor who has all three.

Operations

Dispatch lives in your head and your phone

Assigning drivers, confirming pickups, chasing invoices — every one of these is manual, and every one of them scales with your personal hours, not with the fleet.

Demand

Every month starts at zero

Retail work is one-and-done. A wedding books once. Contract work renews on its own — the airport run at 6am Tuesday happens fifty-two times a year without a single phone call.

08 — The economics

You're clearing $15–20K a month
from ten luxury vehicles.

Let's be direct about what that means. Ten cars, a licensed operation, chauffeurs on payroll, and a strong local name — and the monthly result is roughly what a single senior employee costs. That isn't a reflection of the fleet or the service. It's what happens when a capital-heavy business sells one ride at a time, into a retail market that black-car apps are compressing every year. The number isn't just low. Left alone, it drifts down.

Where you are
$15–20K

Per month, and it swings with the season — prom and wedding months carry the quiet ones. There is no floor under a bad January.

The pressure
Retail is eroding

Every year more one-off airport and night-out work goes to apps. Retail limo demand is the part of your market shrinking fastest — and it's the only part you sell to.

The trap
Assets idle

Ten vehicles depreciate, get insured, and get maintained whether they move or not. Every idle hour is a cost you already paid for.

What changes it
Contract volume

Business accounts fill the idle hours with rides you never had to sell twice — using the same ten cars and the same drivers you already pay for.

Below is the honest version of the argument, and you can test it yourself. The key line is the third one: your fixed costs do not move when the cars do. Once overhead is covered, roughly two thirds of every additional contract fare falls straight through to profit. That is the whole reason a half share of the new business is worth multiples of all of the old one. AXZRO earns nothing until contract volume exists — if the B2B engine doesn't produce, we don't get paid.

Break-even sits at — contract rides per month.

The correction that matters: insurance, vehicle payments, storage, licensing, and admin are fixed — you pay them on a slow month and a busy one. So a new contract ride only costs you a chauffeur, fuel, and wear: about $105. Everything above that drops to profit. This is why filling idle hours moves your bottom line far more than raising prices ever could.

An equal split of net profit — revenue after direct operating costs and platform costs. Not of gross revenue, and not of the value of your vehicles.

AXZRO takes no equity in Famous City Limousine. The fleet, the license, and the brand remain entirely yours.

$0

AXZRO carries the build cost of the platform and the brand. We're paid from profit, which means we only earn when you earn.
